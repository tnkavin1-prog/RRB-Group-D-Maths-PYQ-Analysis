📐 Trigonometry

The Most Important Topic for RRB Group D

📖 1. Concept Overview

What is Trigonometry?

Trigonometry deals with relationships between angles and sides of triangles, especially right-angled triangles.

🎯 What It Means

Study of ratios in right triangles: sin, cos, tan, and their reciprocals

💡 Why It's Used

To find unknown sides or angles when some measurements are given

📍 Where It's Used

Height & distance problems, simplification, value finding

📝 How Examiner Asks

Direct value finding, identity simplification, height-distance applications

⭐ Importance Rating

★★★★★

Frequency: Appeared in 12 out of 76 maths questions (15.8%)

Years Appeared: 2025, 2026 (consistent across all shifts)

Probability of appearing again: Very High (84% confidence)

📚 2. Fundamental Theory

The Right Triangle Foundation

                        / |
                   H   /  | P (Perpendicular)
                      /   |

                    Hypotenuse (H): Longest side, opposite to right angle
                    Perpendicular (P): Side opposite to angle θ
                    Base (B): Side adjacent to angle θ (not the hypotenuse)

The Six Trigonometric Ratios

These are the foundation of ALL trigonometry problems:

Ratio Formula Reciprocal Memory Trick
sin θ P/H cosec θ = H/P Some People Have
cos θ B/H sec θ = H/B Curly Brown Hair
tan θ P/B cot θ = B/P Through Pretty Blue

Alternative: "LALITA" (ललिता) - La (Lamb/Perpendicular), Li (Lamb/Hypotenuse), Ta (Tan/Base) for Hindi speakers

Standard Angle Values (MUST MEMORIZE)

Angle (θ) 0° 30° 45° 60° 90°
sin θ 0 1/2 1/√2 √3/2 1
cos θ 1 √3/2 1/√2 1/2 0
tan θ 0 1/√3 1 √3 ∞
cosec θ ∞ 2 √2 2/√3 1
sec θ 1 2/√3 √2 2 ∞
cot θ ∞ √3 1 1/√3 0
💡 Quick Memory Trick for sin values:
For 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°: Write 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 → Divide by 4 → Take square root
sin 0° = √(0/4) = 0
sin 30° = √(1/4) = 1/2
sin 45° = √(2/4) = 1/√2

📝 3. Complete Formula Sheet

🔹 Basic Identities (Fundamental)

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

This is the Pythagorean identity. Derived from: (P/H)² + (B/H)² = (P² + B²)/H² = H²/H² = 1

1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

Derived by dividing sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by cos²θ

1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

Derived by dividing sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by sin²θ

🔹 Complementary Angles

sin(90° - θ) = cos θ
cos(90° - θ) = sin θ
tan(90° - θ) = cot θ

When angle becomes (90° - θ), function changes to its complement

🔹 Allied Angles

sin(180° - θ) = sin θ
cos(180° - θ) = -cos θ
tan(180° - θ) = -tan θ
sin(-θ) = -sin θ
cos(-θ) = cos θ
tan(-θ) = -tan θ

🔹 Maximum & Minimum Values

-1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1
-1 ≤ cos θ ≤ 1
tan θ can be any real number

🔬 4. Formula Derivations (Step by Step)

Derivation of sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Step 1: In a right triangle, by Pythagoras theorem:

H² = P² + B²

Step 2: We know sin θ = P/H and cos θ = B/H

Step 3: Square both:

sin²θ = P²/H²
cos²θ = B²/H²

Step 4: Add them:

sin²θ + cos²θ = P²/H² + B²/H² = (P² + B²)/H²

Step 5: From Step 1, P² + B² = H²

∴ sin²θ + cos²θ = H²/H² = 1

✓ Hence Proved: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Derivation of 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

Step 1: Start with fundamental identity:

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Step 2: Divide entire equation by cos²θ:

(sin²θ/cos²θ) + (cos²θ/cos²θ) = 1/cos²θ

Step 3: Simplify each term:

tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ

[Since sin θ/cos θ = tan θ and 1/cos θ = sec θ]

✓ Hence Proved: 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

🔍 6. Pattern Recognition (All Variations)

Variation 1: Direct Value Finding ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Pattern: Given a standard angle, find the value of a trigonometric expression

Keywords: "Find the value", "Evaluate", "Calculate"

🟢 Easy Example

Q: Find the value of sin 30° + cos 60°

Step-by-step:

sin 30° = 1/2 (from table)

cos 60° = 1/2 (from table)

Sum = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1

Answer: 1

⚡ Shortcut: Remember sin 30° = cos 60° = 1/2, so sum is always 1

🟡 Medium Example

Q: Evaluate: (sin 45° × cos 45°) + tan 45°

Step-by-step:

sin 45° = 1/√2

cos 45° = 1/√2

tan 45° = 1

(1/√2 × 1/√2) + 1 = 1/2 + 1 = 3/2

Answer: 3/2 or 1.5

⚡ Shortcut: sin θ × cos θ at 45° = (1/√2)² = 1/2 directly

🔴 Hard Example

Q: If sin θ = 3/5, find the value of (cos θ + cosec θ)

Step-by-step:

Given: sin θ = 3/5 = P/H

So P = 3, H = 5

Using Pythagoras: B = √(H² - P²) = √(25 - 9) = √16 = 4

cos θ = B/H = 4/5

cosec θ = H/P = 5/3

cos θ + cosec θ = 4/5 + 5/3 = (12 + 25)/15 = 37/15

Answer: 37/15

⚡ Shortcut: Remember 3-4-5 triangle. If sin = 3/5, then cos = 4/5 immediately

📝 Exam-Level Example (PYQ Style)

Q: What is the value of sin² 30° + cos² 30°?

Method 1 (Calculation):

sin 30° = 1/2, so sin² 30° = 1/4

cos 30° = √3/2, so cos² 30° = 3/4

Sum = 1/4 + 3/4 = 1

Method 2 (Identity - Instant!):

By identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for ANY angle θ

Answer: 1 (solve in 3 seconds!)

❌ Common Mistake: Students waste time calculating individual values when identity gives instant answer

Variation 2: Identity Simplification ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Pattern: Simplify complex trigonometric expressions using identities

Keywords: "Simplify", "Prove that", "Show that"

🟢 Easy Example

Q: Simplify: (1 - sin²θ)

Using identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Therefore: 1 - sin²θ = cos²θ

Answer: cos²θ

🟡 Medium Example

Q: Simplify: (sec²θ - 1)(cosec²θ - 1)

Using identities:

sec²θ - 1 = tan²θ

cosec²θ - 1 = cot²θ

Product = tan²θ × cot²θ

Since cot θ = 1/tan θ, we get tan²θ × (1/tan²θ) = 1

Answer: 1

🔴 Hard Example

Q: If x = a sec θ and y = b tan θ, find x²/a² - y²/b²

x/a = sec θ, so x²/a² = sec²θ

y/b = tan θ, so y²/b² = tan²θ

x²/a² - y²/b² = sec²θ - tan²θ

Using identity: sec²θ - tan²θ = 1

Answer: 1

Variation 3: Height & Distance Application ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Pattern: Real-world problems involving heights, shadows, angles of elevation/depression

Keywords: "Angle of elevation", "Angle of depression", "Shadow", "Tower", "Building"

🟢 Easy Example

Q: A tower casts a shadow of 10 m when sun's altitude is 45°. Find tower's height.

tan 45° = Height/Shadow

1 = Height/10

Height = 10 m

Answer: 10 m

⚡ Shortcut: At 45°, height = shadow length (tan 45° = 1)

🟡 Medium Example

Q: From a point 20 m away from a tower, angle of elevation is 60°. Find height.

tan 60° = Height/Base

√3 = Height/20

Height = 20√3 m

Answer: 20√3 m

🔴 Hard Example

Q: A man observes top of a tower at 30°. After moving 40 m closer, angle becomes 60°. Find tower height.

Let height = h, initial distance = x

From first position: tan 30° = h/x → 1/√3 = h/x → x = h√3

From second position: tan 60° = h/(x-40) → √3 = h/(h√3 - 40)

√3(h√3 - 40) = h

3h - 40√3 = h

2h = 40√3

h = 20√3 m

Answer: 20√3 m

⚡ 7. Power Tricks & Shortcuts

🧮 Mental Math Tricks

  • √3 ≈ 1.732: Remember as "1.732 - Archimedes constant approximation"
  • 1/√2 ≈ 0.707: Half of √2 ≈ 1.414/2 = 0.707
  • 1/√3 ≈ 0.577: Approximately 0.58

🎯 Quick Recognition Tricks

  • If you see sin² + cos² → Answer is ALWAYS 1
  • If sec² - 1 appears → Replace with tan² immediately
  • If cosec² - 1 appears → Replace with cot² immediately
  • At 45°: sin = cos = 1/√2, tan = 1
  • At 30° & 60°: Values swap between sin/cos

🔢 Calculation Shortcuts

  • For 3-4-5 triangle: If sin = 3/5, then cos = 4/5 instantly
  • For 5-12-13 triangle: Common in exams, memorize ratios
  • Multiplication by √3: Multiply by 1.732 or keep as √3 if options allow

❌ 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Confusing sin and cos values

Wrong: Thinking sin 60° = 1/2

Correct: sin 60° = √3/2, sin 30° = 1/2

Fix: Remember sin increases from 0° to 90°

Mistake 2: Sign errors in quadrants

Wrong: cos(180° - θ) = cos θ

Correct: cos(180° - θ) = -cos θ

Fix: Remember "All Silver Tea Cups" (ASTC) rule

Mistake 3: Forgetting reciprocal relationships

Wrong: cosec θ = 1/sin θ but writing sin θ = 1/cosec θ incorrectly

Fix: Practice: sin ↔ cosec, cos ↔ sec, tan ↔ cot

Mistake 4: Not recognizing standard triangles

Wrong: Calculating sides every time

Fix: Memorize 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 triangle ratios

🧠 9. Memory Techniques

Mnemonic 1: Complete Ratio Table

"Some People Have Curly Brown Hair Through Pretty Blue"

Some (Sin) People (Perpendicular) Have (Hypotenuse)
Curly (Cos) Brown (Base) Hair (Hypotenuse)
Through (Tan) Pretty (Perpendicular) Blue (Base)

Mnemonic 2: Sin Value Pattern

Write 0,1,2,3,4 → Divide by 4 → Square root

Works for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°

Mnemonic 3: ASTC Rule (Quadrants)

"All Silver Tea Cups"

1st quadrant (A): All positive
2nd quadrant (S): Sin & cosec positive
3rd quadrant (T): Tan & cot positive
4th quadrant (C): Cos & sec positive

Visualization: Unit Circle

Imagine a clock face. At 3 o'clock (0°): cos=1, sin=0
At 12 o'clock (90°): cos=0, sin=1
This helps remember extreme values
